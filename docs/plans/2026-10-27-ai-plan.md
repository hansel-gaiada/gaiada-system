# The AI plan: routing, model use, department context, usage and cost (AIP-*)

**Status: PLANNED.** Nothing in this document is implemented. It is a measured map of what exists, a
proposed design, tickets, and the questions the owner must answer before anything is built.
**The owner answered all 12 questions on 2026-10-27 (AIP-D1 to AIP-D12, Section 9).** Five answers differ from the defaults (Q2,
Q3, Q6, Q10, Q12), and the tickets they change are marked in Section 6.

**Why this plan exists.** The department-workspaces plan (`docs/plans/2026-10-13-department-workspaces.md`,
section 6, DW-D9 and DW-D17) made the AI Assistant one row in every workspace and kept "today's engine,
threads, streaming, proposals, memory, model picker and routing" unchanged. It deferred three things to a
separate AI plan: **model use**, **department context** and **usage and cost**. This is that plan.

**Binding rules this plan works inside.**

- **Only the Gateway holds provider keys** (root `CLAUDE.md`; `ai-gateway-go/CLAUDE.md`). Every choice
  below keeps that true, and Section 1.6 lists the places where today it is not.
- **A chat runs under the chatting user's principal, and threads stay private to their owner**
  (`docs/blueprints/assistant-foundation.md` section 6). Reaching another person's data needs Cerbos.
- **Personas are deferred to late staging, one per department, optional** (owner, 2026-08-03;
  `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-03-agentic-native-erp-plan.md`, "Deferred", and the persona item that
  says there are zero personas today). Nothing here needs a persona, and nothing here creates one.
- **No seat defaults to Opus** (`ai-agents/CLAUDE.md`). Model choice is a budget decision.

### 1.2 How routing works now

In [`internal/chain/chain.go`](../../ai-gateway-go/internal/chain/chain.go) and `server.go`:

- **First available, healthy provider in the chain wins.** A failure opens a per-provider circuit breaker
  (default 3 failures, 60 s cooldown) and the next provider is tried. A 429 opens it for the Retry-After window.
- **A caller can send one hint, `provider`**, on both `POST /complete` and `POST /complete/stream`. It only
  **reorders** the chain; an unknown or unavailable name falls through silently (`RunWithHint`).
- **There is no model hint.** Each provider has exactly one fixed model from its environment variable. A caller
  cannot ask for "a cheaper model" or "a stronger model" on the same provider.
- **There is no task hint.** Nothing tells the gateway whether a request is a chat reply, a thread-title, a
  compaction summary, an agent step or a DLP check. Every call is treated the same.
- The chain order and caps can be changed at runtime by the admin console (`PUT /admin/config`,
  [`internal/adminconfig/adminconfig.go`](../../ai-gateway-go/internal/adminconfig/adminconfig.go)); provider
  credentials and the egress allowlist cannot (env and restart only, by design).
- **Safety layers on every call:** fail-closed bearer auth, a DLP scrub of the prompt (card and national-ID
  patterns, [`internal/dlp/`](../../ai-gateway-go/internal/dlp/)), an optional local model classifier
  (`DLP_CLASSIFIER_ENABLED`, off by default), a response-side stream scrubber, a dial-time egress allowlist
  ([`internal/egress/`](../../ai-gateway-go/internal/egress/)), and an egress audit line per request.

### 1.3 What the brain picker does

- UI: [`BrainPicker.tsx`](../../platform-ui/src/components/assistant/BrainPicker.tsx), options in
  `BRAIN_OPTIONS` in [`platform-ui/src/lib/assistant.ts`](../../platform-ui/src/lib/assistant.ts):
  Auto (failover chain), Ollama (local), "Ollama Cloud" (the `openai` slot), Hermes, Gemini, Claude.
- It is **per thread**. The choice is stored as `assistant_threads.brain_provider` and sent as the gateway's
  `provider` hint on every later reply in that thread.
- It is **a hint, never a requirement.** The reply carries a "served by" badge from the gateway's `meta` event,
  which always names the real server.
- **Known mismatch, not a bug in the picker:** the option labelled "Ollama Cloud" is really whatever
  `OPENAI_BASE_URL` points at. Today that is OpenRouter (GLM 5.3 Flash), so the label is wrong on the live
  box. The Gemini and Claude options are inert while their keys are empty, and "Hermes" does nothing unless
  `hermes` is in `LLM_CHAIN` (`BRAIN_OPTIONS` comment and the compose comment). Most of the picker is therefore
  a no-op today.
- The picker is shown to every user who can open the assistant. There is no per-role or per-department limit
  on which brain a person may choose [no limit found in `assistant.controller.ts`].

### 1.5 What department context the assistant gets today

- The prompt is **one string**: a generic system preamble, the thread's rolling summary, the recent transcript,
  the user's **confirmed** memory, and up to a few retrieved knowledge chunks
  ([`assistant/context.ts`](../../platform-nest/src/modules/assistant/context.ts)). The preamble is explicitly
  generic: "no per-department persona".
- **Page context** (ASST-22, `pageContextPrefix`) is a short preamble naming the record the user was looking at.
  The workspaces plan adds a department chip and suggested prompts, and says they "send nothing different to
  the model".
- **Knowledge retrieval** runs as the chatting user: candidates from the store, then DW-D26 filters them with
  the per-record read check as the thread owner, plus public-tier hits
  (`retrieveReadableKnowledge`). So an assistant citation never shows a record the person could not open.
- The knowledge store has an `acl text[]` column and a two-tier `audience` (public website corpus, internal ERP
  records), with tenant as the pre-filter ([`ai-agents/src/knowledge/store.ts`](../../ai-agents/src/knowledge/store.ts),
  [`modules/knowledge/README.md`](../../platform-nest/src/modules/knowledge/README.md)). **There is no department
  dimension**: the filter is the company plus the record-level read check.
- Tools run through the MCP hub as the chatting user, so tool reads are authorized per record by Cerbos already.
- Memory is owner-only. Threads are private to (owner, company).

**So today the assistant knows the company and the person, not the department**, except through what the person
can open.

## 2. Model use and routing

### 2.1 The options

| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. One fixed default** | Every call uses the first chain provider (today's Auto) | Simplest; already works | The same model answers a title and a hard analysis; cost and quality are both wrong somewhere; no basis for budgets |
| **B. Per-task routing** | The caller names a **task class**; the gateway maps it to a cheap or strong model | The biggest cost saving for the least user effort. Titles, compaction, classification and triage go cheap; reasoning and drafting go strong | Needs a new gateway field and an admin table; needs a few evals so "cheap" is safe |
| **C. Per-department defaults** | Each department gets its own default tier or model | Matches how departments differ (SEO drafting versus HR policy) | Needs a store and an admin screen; premature without usage data; risks a model zoo |
| **D. User choice within limits** | A person picks a tier from a list the company allows | Respects the picker we already have; power users get control | A free choice is a cost lever the user holds; needs an allow-list and, at a cap, a rule |

### 2.2 Recommendation: B first, then a thin D, C only when the data asks for it

1. **Add a task class and a tier to the gateway call, not a model name.** The gateway chooses the model; the
   caller states intent. Proposed classes: `chat`, `summarise` (compaction, titles), `classify` (DLP, triage),
   `agent_step`, `draft`, `embed` (already a separate chain). Proposed tiers: `fast`, `standard`, `strong`.
   An admin table (in the existing runtime-config override file) maps class to tier and tier to
   `provider + model`. Keys still live only in the gateway; the model name is data in that table.
2. **The `provider` hint stays**, unchanged and ranked **above** the tier, so nothing that exists today breaks
   (the contract file says byte-for-byte parity is binding).
3. **The brain picker becomes Auto, Fast, Strong**, plus the named providers for people holding an
   "advanced" grant. This is honest because a named provider that has no key today is a no-op; the picker should
   list only providers the gateway reports as available (`GET /health` already reports them).
4. **Hermes leaves the picker** until it is behind the gateway (Section 1.6, finding 1).
5. **Per-department defaults (C) are deferred.** Decide after two weeks of real usage data from the new ledger
   (Section 4). Until then a department is just a reporting dimension, not a routing one.
6. **The default stays cheap.** The only live model today is GLM 5.3 Flash through OpenRouter. Routing is
   only useful once a second tier exists (an owner decision on keys and models; see question Q1).

**Why this order.** B needs no new UI and no new identity model; it cuts cost from day one, and it produces the
per-task data that D and C would need. It also fits the rule: the gateway chooses, the gateway holds the keys.

**What stays out.** No new model provider, no fine-tune and no local-model eval work here. The model registry
and eval gate in `ai-agents/` (D13) already govern which model may serve a write agent; this plan reuses them
and does not widen them.

## 3. Department context

### 3.1 What "real context" means

The workspaces plan says the department chip and suggested prompts "send nothing different to the model".
This plan changes that, in three layers, each added only when the one before it is proven:

| Layer | What the model sees | Where the data comes from | Who is the principal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Where you are** | One line: the department, the page, the record ("You are helping someone in the SEO department, on the Rankings page") | The workspace registry (`platform-ui/src/lib/workspaces.ts`) and the existing page-context preamble | The chatting user; carries no data, only a label |
| **2. Department knowledge, scoped retrieval** | Knowledge chunks relevant to the question, from the department's own documents first | The knowledge store, filtered by company **and** department scope | The chatting user, through the existing per-record read filter (DW-D26) |
| **3. The person's own scope** | What the person's tools can read: their department's tasks, requests, reports | MCP hub tools, as today | The chatting user, Cerbos per record |

### 3.2 How the department enters retrieval

- Give each knowledge document an optional **department tag** (a new column, or use the existing `acl` scope
  strings, which already express "readable by this scope"; the first is clearer, the second needs no migration.
  See question Q4).
- Retrieval ranks the current department's documents first and still returns company-wide documents; it never
  widens to another department's documents the person could not already open.
- The department's runbooks and SOPs are the first corpus. The `persona/dept-pm/` documents show the shape
  (identity, boundaries, runbooks) but are **not** loaded as a persona; they would be ingested as plain
  knowledge documents tagged `pm`, so no persona is created.
- A per-department **system preamble** (short, owner-editable text in the workspace registry) states the
  department's vocabulary and boundaries. It is plain configuration, not an identity, and it does not
  contradict the deferral of personas: a persona is a `users` row with roles; this is a paragraph.

### 3.3 The authorization line (binding)

1. **A chat runs as the chatting user.** The principal for retrieval and tools is the user's own, never a
   service principal and never the department's.
2. **Department context is a filter that narrows, never an authority that widens.** The model may see a
   department's documents only if the existing record-level read check passes for this user. The department tag
   decides ranking, not access.
3. **Threads stay owner-private.** Department context is assembled per turn and is not stored in a shared place.
   A head reading "the department's AI activity" sees counts and cost (Section 4), never thread text. Reading
   another person's thread remains the separate audited action the blueprint already defines.
4. **Reaching another person's data needs Cerbos.** For example, "summarise my team's overdue requests" is a tool
   call that Cerbos authorizes per record for the asking user. If it is denied, the answer says it could not read
   it; it does not fall back to a broader path. A new capability here (a head asking about a report's work) must
   be a registered tool with its own Cerbos resource, not a prompt trick. An unlisted resource kind is a silent
   deny in this estate, so each new kind is a named ticket.
5. **No cross-person data in a shared prompt.** Compaction summaries and memory are per owner already. A
   department-level "shared memory" is **out of scope** and needs an owner ruling and its own plan.
6. **DLP stays on every call**, and the department preamble and chunks pass through the same scrub.

### 3.4 Agents per department

`ai-agents/` specialists are global today (`specialists.ts`). Per-department agents are a **visibility**
question first (which specialists a department's workspace lists, and which may a person hand work to) and an
**identity** question second (personas, deferred). This plan does the first only: a registry field
"departments this specialist is shown in", so the roster in the Details panel shows relevant agents. No agent
gets its own identity. The eval rule in `ai-agents/CLAUDE.md` still applies: an agent change needs an eval case first.

## 4. Usage and cost

### 4.1 What to meter

One row per gateway call that reaches a provider, written by the **platform**, because only the platform knows
the person, the department and the thread. The gateway reports facts; it does not know people (it only sees the
company header).

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Time, company (tenant), user, department (when known) | The caller (platform-nest, ai-agents runner, knowledge, search) passes them; the gateway echoes the ids it was given |
| Capability, task class, tier | The new routing fields (Section 2) |
| Provider and model that served | Already in the gateway's `meta` event |
| Prompt tokens, completion tokens, and **whether real or estimated** | Gateway `usage` event. The live `openai` provider must be taught to report them (ticket AIP-02); until then, labelled estimates, as the assistant already does |
| Cost in USD, and whether it came from the provider or from a price table | OpenRouter returns usage and cost in its responses [UNVERIFIED: not read in the code today]; otherwise a small price table per `provider + model` |
| Outcome (ok, blocked by DLP, blocked by cap, failed over, error) | Gateway, as today in the audit row |
| Source surface (assistant, agent, compaction, knowledge, search, bot) | The caller |

**Never store prompt or answer text in this table.** The egress audit already follows that rule; keep it.

### 4.2 Where it lives

- A new table `ai_usage_events` in the platform database (company-scoped, with row-level security like the
  other tenant tables), append-only, with an index on (company, department, day) and (company, user, day).
  A daily roll-up view feeds the screens.
- **Why not the gateway's JSONL audit:** it has no tenant, user or tokens, it is a file on one box, and it
  restarts empty. It stays as the security record. The new table is the business record.
- **Why not the existing `assistant_threads.total_cost_usd`:** it covers only assistant threads, is never
  written, and misses agents and compaction. The columns can be filled from the new table later, or left.
- **Budget counters move out of memory.** Today the daily call count resets on restart. The cap check should read
  the same durable source (or be seeded from it at boot), so a deploy cannot hand out a fresh allowance.
- The search ledger (`modules/search/providers/ledger.ts`) is the pattern to copy for "incurred" rows: a call
  that was charged but delivered nothing is still recorded.

### 4.3 Who sees what

| Viewer | Sees | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Any person | Their **own** usage this month: tokens, cost estimate, calls | Own rows only |
| A department head | Their department's totals by day, by surface, by model, and **per-person totals within the department** | The existing head scope (a head is an active position with the `org_unit_lead` role); counts and cost only, never text |
| The owner and the GM | Company-wide, per department and per model; the cap settings | Cerbos on a new resource kind `ai_usage` (a **new kind is six artifacts**, so it is a named ticket) |
| IT, for operations | Provider health, failover rate, cap hits; not per-person cost | Existing monitoring access |

The department head's view is one tab in the workspace (candidate name: "AI usage", next to the other head
views from DW-4), not a new top-level page. Per-person figures inside a department are visible to that head and
the owner only. [Q6 asks whether a person should also see their own rank against peers; the default is no.]

**Changed by the owner on 2026-10-30 (AIP-D18, AIP-D19).** A person sees their own usage again (AIP-05 is back). The owner, the GM,
company admins and the superadmin see the company view (totals, no per-person lines). Per-person lines inside a department stay with that
department's head and the owner (AIP-D6). The runaway alert names the person to IT as well as to the owner. Section 6.3 is the rule now.

### 4.4 Budgets and caps, and what happens at a cap

| Level | Proposed default | What happens at the cap |
|---|---|---|
| **Gateway global** (exists) | The existing daily call cap, durable | 429 "degraded until tomorrow" (today's behaviour). Keep, and show it in the UI as a clear message, not an error |
| **Company** | A monthly USD budget set by the owner | At 80 percent: a notice to the owner and the GM. At 100 percent: **soft stop** (below) |
| **Department** | A monthly USD budget, default "none" (report only) | At 80 percent: a notice to the head. At 100 percent: the same soft stop for that department, which the head or the owner can lift |
| **Person** | A daily message allowance, default generous | At the allowance: a friendly "you have used today's allowance" with the reset time; the owner can raise it per person |

**Soft stop, not a wall.** At a cap the assistant **downshifts** instead of failing: the tier drops to `fast`
(the cheapest), long-context features such as large knowledge retrieval are trimmed, and agent runs are
queued for approval rather than started. Only at the hard company cap does it return the gateway's degraded
message. A DR-burst override already exists in the gateway (`POST /admin/dr-mode`) and stays the owner's only
way to lift the hard cap.

**Cost runaway guards** (apply regardless of budgets): a per-request maximum prompt size, a maximum number of
agent steps per run (exists), and an alert when one person or one agent exceeds, say, five times their own daily
average. The alert goes to IT and the owner.

## 5. Knowledge, AI Agents and The Office: where they live

Per the workspaces plan (the mapping table near line 1287, DW-D17), they sit under **Company** for now:
**Company > Knowledge**, **Company > AI Agents**, **Company > The Office** (rows in `platform-ui/src/lib/workspaces.ts`,
lines ~1154 to 1157). This plan keeps that, and adds only what it needs.

| Item | After this plan | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Assistant** | Stays a row in every workspace (DW-D9). Gains the department chip's real context (Section 3), the tier picker, and a small "your usage" line in the Details panel | The one place a person talks to AI |
| **Knowledge** | Stays in **Company** as the library. Gains the department tag on documents and a "show this department's documents" filter. Each workspace gets **no new row**; its AI Assistant reads the department's slice | Moving it into every workspace would create ten copies of one library |

Revisit with AIP-06's tier table if it matters.

**The design.**

1. **A chat endpoint for registered agents only.** `POST /v1/chat/completions` in `ai-gateway-go`, in the OpenAI chat-completions shape.
   - **Who may call it.** Each agent has its own bearer token. The gateway stores only token hashes, from `GATEWAY_AGENT_TOKENS` (`name:sha256hex,...`). The shared `GATEWAY_TOKEN` does not open this route, and an agent token opens nothing else (not `/complete`, `/embed`, `/media` or `/admin/*`).
   - **What is forwarded.** The request body is an allow-list:
     - `model`, `messages`, `tools`, `tool_choice`, `temperature`, `top_p`, `max_tokens` or `max_completion_tokens`, `stop`, `stream`;
     - inside `messages`: the roles `system`, `user`, `assistant` and `tool`; text content; an assistant's `tool_calls`; a tool message's `tool_call_id`.

     Unknown top-level keys are dropped (their names are logged), never forwarded. Image or audio content parts are refused with 400: DLP cannot read them.
   - **Model choice.** The caller's `model` never chooses a provider. The gateway serves from the first chat-capable provider in `LLM_CHAIN` that is available, and the response's `model` names what served.
   - **No echo.** `echo` never serves this route: an agent that gets an echo 200 acts on nonsense. With no chat-capable provider available the answer is 503 in the OpenAI error shape.
2. **The same controls as `/complete`.**
   - **DLP.** `dlp.DLP` runs on every text the caller sends: each message's content, tool results, and the arguments of earlier tool calls. The classifier runs too, when one is active. Tool definitions are code and are not scrubbed. A block is the same 503 `/complete` gives.
   - **Budget.** Each call is a `b.Take("agent:<name>", now)`: the agent's own daily cap (from the per-tenant cap settings) and the global cap. Agents may never use the last `GATEWAY_AGENT_RESERVE_PCT` of the global cap (default 25), which stays for the ERP's own users.
   - **Audit.** One egress audit row per call: capability `chat`, the agent's name, the provider, token counts when the provider reports them, latency and blocked reason. Never any message text.
3. **Streaming.** `stream: true` relays the provider's server-sent events, tool-call deltas included, with the same output handling `/complete/stream` applies. A non-streaming answer is the provider's JSON with `model` restated.
4. **Reachable only over the mesh, never on a public address.**
   - The gateway serves this route on a second listener (`GATEWAY_AGENT_LISTEN`, default off). That listener serves only `/v1/chat/completions` and a minimal `/v1/models`.
   - Compose publishes it on `127.0.0.1:3012` only. A host proxy on `gda-aicenter` listens on `10.88.0.1:3012` and forwards to it; the proxy's systemd unit starts after `wg-quick@wg0`. Binding a container port to the mesh address directly would stop the gateway starting after a reboot that raises `wg0` late (the Postgres-before-`docker0` trap).
   - `ufw` admits `10.88.0.3` to `10.88.0.1:3012` and nothing else.
   - The WireGuard routes between `helios` and `gda-aicenter` go through the `sumopod` hub.
   - WireGuard encrypts the hop, so the listener speaks plain HTTP.
5. **On `helios`, per agent.** First back up `config.yaml`, `.env` and `auth.json`. Then:
   1. Set the model to a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint, `base_url: http://10.88.0.1:3012/v1`, with the agent's own token as its key.
   2. Point the auxiliary tasks (vision, web summaries and the rest) at the same endpoint, or turn them off.
   3. Restart the agent.
   4. Prove a prompt end to end **and** see the gateway's audit row naming the agent.
   5. Only then delete the agent's own provider credentials: `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` and `GEMINI_API_KEY` for `hermes-seo`, the OpenRouter credential in `auth.json` for `hermes-bsc`.

   `SEMRUSH_API_KEY`, `FIGMA_API_KEY` and the Search Console OAuth secret are tool credentials, not model keys, and stay. Rollback is the backups. `helios` is a production delivery host and these are `azlan`'s agents, so the orchestrator writes the commands and the owner runs them.

### 6.2 Phase 3 design (session gaiada-system-49, 2026-10-29)

**Status: BUILT and LIVE, Phase 3 COMPLETE (2026-10-29).** Train 1 (AIP-10 to 14) shipped in 1.0.0-alpha.463, train 2 (AIP-13b) in 1.0.0-alpha.465. The switch has been on for every company since 2026-10-29 (AIP-D17), and all six tickets were proven live that day (`TASKS.md`, program `ai-plan`).

Section 3 is the rule; this is how it is built. Two trains: **train 1** is AIP-10, 11, 12, 13 and 14; **train 2** is AIP-13b.

**1. The department of a turn (AIP-10).**

- With each message, the UI sends `context: { departmentId, page }`. These are what the drawer and the full page already
  work out for the department chip (DW-2h, `resolveAssistantOrigin`): the org node id of the workspace the assistant was
  opened from, and the registry's name for the page. Since DW-7d HR, Finance and IT have node ids too.
- The server does not trust either value as a fact:
  - `departmentId` must be an active `org_units` row of the thread's company; the server reads the name and the
    workspace key (`deptSlug` of the name) from that row. An unknown id means no department, and no error.
  - `page` is a label only. It is trimmed, kept to one line and at most 60 characters, and must use letters, digits,
    spaces and `&·-/'()`; anything else drops it.
- The resolved context is written on the reply's placeholder row as a `turn_context` part, the way ASST-17 writes
  `turn_mode`. The stream reads it from the row, never from the query string.
- It is a per-turn fact. Nothing is stored on the thread, so the next message sent from another page carries that page.
- `POST assistant/ask` takes the same optional `context`.

**2. One flag per company.** `ASSISTANT_DEPT_CONTEXT` on platform-nest:

- empty (the default) means off for every company;
- `all` means on for every company;
- a comma-separated list of company ids means on for those companies only.

When it is off, the prompt and the retrieval call are byte for byte today's. The one flag covers AIP-10, 12 and 13. The
release ships it off; turning it on for a company is the owner's call.

**3. The prompt (AIP-10, AIP-13).** Two parts follow the generic preamble:

- the context line: "You are helping someone in the SEO department, on the Rankings page." (or without the page part);
- then "Department guidance:" and the department's preamble.

The preamble's source:

- **AIP-13:** a short default per workspace key, in `platform-nest/src/modules/assistant/department-context.ts`. It
  describes the department's work and vocabulary, taken from the registry's mission line, and invents no rules.
  Unknown departments get no preamble.
- **AIP-13b:** the company's own row for that unit replaces the default.

The cap is 1,000 characters. The gateway's DLP scrubs the whole prompt, as today. **Why on the server, not in
`workspaces.ts` as the table above says:** AIP-D10 lets a preamble name rules, and a rule that the browser sends is a
rule that the browser can leave out.

**4. Knowledge gets a department (AIP-11, AIP-D4).**

- `knowledge_chunks.department_id text NULL` holds the org node id, the same shape as `projects.department_id`, with an
  index on `(tenant_id, department_id)`. The store's own `init()` adds it with `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`, as it added
  `audience`.
- `/ingest` takes `departmentId`.
- The ERP source sets it per record:
  - a project: its own `department_id`;
  - a core or PM task, a deliverable, a PM doc: its project's;
  - a meeting: its own, or else its project's;
  - a file: its parent project's, or its parent task's project's;
  - clients, reports, org and people: none.
- The chunk text does not change, so nothing is re-embedded. The store's reuse path rewrites the metadata of the rows
  it keeps, so **the next sweep is the backfill** (about 3 minutes).

**5. Ranking (AIP-12). It narrows, never widens (Section 3.3).**

- `/search` takes `department: { tenantId, nodeId }`.
- Inside the store's SQL, the WHERE pre-filter is unchanged. After it, a chunk of that company and that department has
  its score multiplied by `KNOWLEDGE_DEPARTMENT_BOOST` (default 1.25, clamped to 1 to 2).
- It is a boost, not a partition. A much more relevant company document still outranks a weak department one.
- Hits carry `departmentId`.
- In platform-nest, DW-D26's `filterReadableHits` runs on the boosted candidates exactly as before.
- The adversarial tests prove three things:
  - a department record that the person cannot open is never cited, even when it is the top boosted hit;
  - with the flag off, the search call carries no department;
  - another company's chunk with the same node id (node ids repeat across companies) is never cited.

**6. Runbooks (AIP-13's second half).** The repo holds no department runbooks:

- `persona/dept-pm/runbooks/` is empty by design;
- the rest of `persona/` is identity and voice material, which must not become a persona.

A department's runbooks and SOPs are therefore ordinary ERP documents in a project the department owns: the project's
Docs tab, or files attached to it. AIP-11 tags them, so they are ingested and ranked first with no new source. Nothing
in `persona/` is ingested.

**7. Heads edit their preamble (AIP-13b, train 2).**

- A table `assistant_dept_preambles`:
  - columns: company, `unit_node_id`, `body` (at most 1,000 characters), `updated_by`, `updated_at`;
  - one row per (company, unit);
  - FORCE RLS, the house core pattern.
- A new Cerbos kind `dept_assistant_preamble`, which is six artifacts. Its actions are `read` and `edit`, shaped exactly
  like `dept_notice`'s unit rules:
  - `company_admin` may read and edit;
  - so may the `org_unit_lead` of the unit or of an enclosing unit, in its own rule, with `unitAncestors` fed by the
    handler;
  - the tenant trap stays closed;
  - automation and bots are denied `edit`;
  - the permission arm covers `read` only.
- Every edit writes an activity row.
- The edit screen in the department workspace shows the text exactly as the assistant reads it: the context line plus
  the preamble.
- Opus reviews the Cerbos rule.

**8. The roster (AIP-14).**

- `AgentDef.departments?: string[]` holds workspace keys; an absent field means every department.
- The runner's registry returns it, and `GET assistant/agents` passes it on.
- The Details panel lists the current department's agents and the general ones.
- No agent gets an identity, and no agent's behaviour changes. `ai-agents/CLAUDE.md`'s eval-case rule is for behaviour,
  so it is not triggered.

1.0.0-alpha.469; AIP-16, AIP-05 and AIP-17a in 470 (17a proven when 471's deploy restarted the gateway and it restored the day's count); AIP-17b
and AIP-19 in 472; AIP-18 with its screens and the owner's budget forms in 475. The plan below is what was built; `TASKS.md` (program `ai-plan`)
holds each box's live evidence. It was planned as three trains: **train 1** is AIP-15a, 15, 16 and 05 (the reads and the screens); **train 2** is AIP-17a, 17b and 19
(the durable cap, budgets, notices and the runaway alert); **train 3** is AIP-18 (the soft stop). A box ticks only after a live check.

**0. Measured first (live, 2026-10-30, read-only, counts only).**

- 41,993 usage rows since 2026-10-27, from 2 people in 3 companies: 41,976 knowledge embeds, 14 assistant replies, 1 agent run
  and 2 others. The total cost is $0.00106, all of it from the 14 replies, priced by the provider.
- **No row has a department: 0 of 41,993.** The writer fills `org_unit_id` only when the person's primary placement is an
  `org_units` row. Only 2 departments have one, and only 6 of 33 primary placements map to one. Phase 3 hit the same fact
  (departments resolve from `org_units` or from `company_org_structure`). A department view built on `org_unit_id` would be empty.
- Embeds outnumber replies about 3,000 to 1. Every view therefore splits by surface; one "calls" total would mean nothing.

**1. Which department a row belongs to (AIP-15a).**

- A new column `ai_usage_events.unit_node_id text`: the org node id of the person's primary placement on the day of the call
  (`org_unit_memberships.unit_node_id`), whether or not that node has an `org_units` row. It has the same shape as
  `projects.department_id` and knowledge's `department_id`, and it is capped at 120 characters like the other labels.
- The append-only trigger already refuses any change except clearing `user_id` or `org_unit_id`, so the new column can never
  change. Erasure keeps it, because a department is not personal data.
- **The bill goes to the person's home department, not to the page the turn came from** (AIP-10's context). A budget belongs to
  the department the person works in. A head of SEO who asks from the Web Dev workspace spends SEO's budget.
- Both writers fill it: platform-nest's `recordGatewayCall` and the ingest route (AIP-03b). `org_unit_id` is filled as today.
- No backfill. The old rows are shown as "No department recorded". The table is append-only by design, and only 15 rows of two
  people on mock data would gain a department.
- A row with no person (most knowledge embeds) has no department. The company view shows those rows by surface, as "Company,
  no person".

**2. The Cerbos kind `ai_usage` (AIP-15).** It is six artifacts plus the four pinned counts. One resource is one company's usage,
narrowed to one unit when the handler feeds `unitAncestors` (computed with the tenant, as AIP-13b does).

| Action | What it opens | Who (AIP-D6, AIP-D18) | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| `read_own` | The caller's own figures | Every staff member of the company | A role rule for `company_admin`, `manager`, `member` and `viewer` with `inTenant`. The route reads only `user_id = caller` |
| `read_unit` | One department's totals **and its per-person lines** | The `org_unit_lead` of the unit or of an enclosing unit | Its own rule, over `unitAncestors`. No mirror, because a lead reaches it |
| `read_company` | Company totals by department, model, surface and day. **No per-person lines** | `company_admin` (role rule), the owner (mirror; the owner mirrors `company_admin`), the GM (seat), the superadmin (wildcard) | The GM is a seat, not a grant. The handler feeds `gmIds`, the lead-seat holders of the company's GM unit (`gmUnitIds` and `loadSeatHoldersByNode`, the SCP-1b shape), to a new derived role `ai_usage_gm_seat` in its own rule |
| `read_people` | Per-person lines in every department | The owner only (the key is in `OWNER_EXTENSION` with `uiGrantable: false`, the AIP-D16 pattern) and the superadmin | The mirror; no role rule |
| `manage` | Budgets, the company alert figure, department modes, person allowances, lifting any stop | The owner only (`OWNER_EXTENSION`) and the superadmin | The mirror; no role rule |
| `lift_stop` | Lifting one department's soft stop for the rest of the month | The unit's `org_unit_lead` | Its own rule. The handler asks `manage` first (AIP-13b's key split) |

- Bots and automation are denied `manage` and `lift_stop`, in the `dept_notice` shape.
- The superadmin is `platform_admin`'s wildcard, as on every kind. AIP-D16 and AIP-D18 both name the superadmin.
- **Nobody sees a peer.** A member reads only their own line. A head reads the people in their own subtree. The GM and company
  admins read totals, never people. Only the owner and the superadmin read every person.
- No text is ever shown, because the table has no column that could hold any.
- Who sets budgets is not in a ruling. AIP-D7 says "a figure the owner sets", and AIP-D9 says the owner raises an allowance, so
  `manage` is the owner's alone. Widening it later is one role rule.

**3. The read routes (AIP-15).** Every route works by month: the UTC calendar month, matching the usage view's UTC days
(`REPORTS_TZ` is unset live).

- `GET :t/ai-usage/me?month=YYYY-MM` needs `read_own`. It returns the caller's calls, tokens and cost by surface and by day.
- `GET :t/ai-usage/units/:unitNodeId?month=`:
  - with `read_people` or `read_unit`: the unit's totals plus `people[]` (name, calls, tokens, cost);
  - with only `read_company`: the same totals and no `people` key;
  - otherwise 403.

  A unit counts its whole subtree: a division's rows add to its department.
- `GET :t/ai-usage/company?month=` needs `read_company` or `read_people`. It returns totals by department (each linking to its unit
  view), by model, by surface and by day. The budget lines are added when AIP-17b lands.
- Every figure says what it is:
  - tokens are marked when any row was an estimate;
  - cost is marked `provider` or `table`;
  - a missing figure is unknown, never 0 (the AIP-01 rule).
- Each answer carries `capabilities` (`canSeePeople`, `canManage`, `canLiftStop`) for the screens.

**4. The screens (AIP-16, AIP-05).** Every screen shows counts and money only: no text, no thread titles.

Both department tabs go in the Performance block's **Analytics** row, which already pairs "My activity" (everyone) with
"Team activity" (the head). Not the AI Assistant row: its first tab opens the drawer over the current page, so no page draws that
row's tab strip and a second tab there cannot be found (found in AIP-13d's live walk; "Department guidance" moved off it for the same
reason).

- **Your usage (AIP-05, reinstated by AIP-D18).** Analytics › "My AI use", `/analytics/ai-usage`, ungated: the person's own month.
- **The department's usage.** Analytics › "AI usage", `/analytics/ai-usage/department?unit={dept}`, offered with
  `unitCap: "ai_usage.read_unit"` or the company read. It shows the unit's month: totals, by surface and model, and per person when
  allowed.
- **The company view.** `/ai-usage` in the Company area (`areas.ts`), beside Knowledge and Agents. It is for the owner, the GM,
  company admins and the superadmin.

**5. The gateway's cap survives a restart (AIP-17a).**

- `ai-gateway-go/internal/budget` writes its day counters (global, per company, embed, per agent) to `data/budget-state.json` on
  the existing `gateway-data` volume.
- The write is atomic (a temp file, then a rename, the `adminconfig` pattern). It happens at most every 5 seconds while the counts
  change, and once on shutdown.
- At boot, the gateway loads the file when its day is today (UTC) and starts from those counts. A deploy or a restart no longer
  hands out a fresh allowance. A crash can lose up to 5 seconds of counts; that is accepted.
- DR mode and `SetCaps` behave as before. The file holds counts, not caps.
- **Why not the usage table**, as the ticket first said:
  - the gateway must not depend on the platform's database;
  - the table does not hold every gateway call: the `helios` agents' calls write no usage rows.

  The gateway's own count is the right source for the gateway's own cap.

**6. Budgets and allowances (AIP-17b).** Two FORCE RLS tables in the house core pattern:

- `ai_budgets`:
  - one row per company (`scope 'company'`): the alert figure in USD a month. It has no mode, because AIP-D8 allows no
    company-level stop;
  - one row per department that has a budget (`scope 'unit'`, `unit_node_id`): USD a month, and `mode` `report` (the default,
    AIP-D7) or `soft_stop`, plus `stop_lifted_month` when a stop is lifted.
- `ai_person_allowances` holds per-person overrides of the daily reply allowance. With no row, the allowance is the default of 200
  (AIP-D9, `AI_DAILY_REPLY_ALLOWANCE`).
- Only `manage` edits either table: the budgets from the company view, an allowance from a person's line. Every edit writes an
  activity row.
- Month-to-date spend is the sum of `cost_usd` over the unit's subtree this UTC month. Rows with no cost are counted separately as
  unknown.

**7. Notices at 80 and 100 percent (AIP-17b; report only first).** A platform job runs every 10 minutes:

- it computes month-to-date spend for each company with an alert figure and each department with a budget;
- it notifies once per threshold per month, through `notify()`, with a dedupe table keyed by company, scope, unit, month and
  threshold:
  - for a department: its head or heads, and the owner;
  - for the company figure: the owner and the GM (Section 4.4).

In `report` mode, that is all that happens.

**8. The runaway alert (AIP-19, AIP-D19).** The same job checks two things:

- a person whose calls today, on every surface, exceed 5 times their own daily average over the last 14 days. It needs at least 7
  active days and at least 20 calls today, so a new user is not flagged on day one;
- an agent whose runs today exceed 5 times its own daily average (surface `agent`, by `task`, the agent's name).

Once per person or agent per day, a notice **names the person** (AIP-D19) with today's calls and their usual daily figure. It goes
to the owner and to IT: the lead-seat holders of the company's IT unit (slug `it`, found the way the GM is found).

**9. The soft stop (AIP-18, AIP-D8; train 3).** At send time, the assistant asks one cached function, `budgetState(tenant, user)`
(cached 60 seconds per company, unit and person). It reports a stop when either holds:

- **the person is over their allowance:** today's assistant replies (surface `assistant`, outcome `ok`) are at or above it;
- **their home department is stopped:** its mode is `soft_stop`, month-to-date spend is at or above its budget, and the stop was not
  lifted this month.

When a stop holds, the turn:

- runs on the `fast` tier, overriding the thread's tier and any named-provider hint for this turn only;
- retrieves fewer knowledge passages, with a smaller context budget;
- starts no agent runs. A hand-off is saved with a new status, `held`. Adding it changes the CHECK on `assistant_handoffs.status`
  (0084), written as a full redeclare because of the shared-CHECK trap. A held hand-off starts when the stop is lifted
  (`lift_stop` or `manage`) or when the next period begins (the next month for a department, the next day for an allowance). The
  person can cancel it;
- shows one line in the chat, for example "You have reached today's AI allowance; replies continue in a lighter mode until 00:00
  UTC", or "Your department has reached its AI budget for October; replies continue in a lighter mode".

**The honest consequence of AIP-D1:** while every tier is the same model, the `fast` tier costs the same. Until a second model is
approved, the stop's real effects are the trimmed retrieval, the held agent runs and the line in the chat. The gateway's global cap
stays the only hard stop (429, "degraded until tomorrow"), unchanged.
