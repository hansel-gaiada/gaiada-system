## 2. What the surface enforces, and who enforces it

The controller deliberately owns no invariant except one: it derives `unitAncestors` **server-side
from the closure table**, never from the request body, because a caller-supplied ancestry is a
caller-supplied authorization decision. Everything else is Cerbos's or the choke point's:

| # (design §6.3) | Invariant | Enforced by | Proven by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Subtree bound | Cerbos `role_grant` + server-derived ancestry | dept head grants inside, 403 outside |
| 2 | Ceiling (subset of grantor's perms) | `assertWithinCeiling` | dept head cannot grant `hr_manager` |
| 3 | Allow-list | `assertRoleUiGrantable` | the `client` case |
| 4 | Scope validity | `assertRoleScopeAllowed` | `platform_admin` @ company refused |
| 5 | No self-target | Cerbos structural DENY (403), mirrored as a 400 | self-grant refused **for company_admin** |
| 6 | Elevated fence | `assertNotElevated` | see §3.3 — it is shadowed, honestly recorded |
| 7 | Sensitive routing | this controller | refused as `override_required`, see §4 |

Plus two of its own: a target outside the company is refused, and a **reconciler-managed grant is not
hand-revocable** (the next reconcile would restore it, so the revoke would appear to work and silently
undo itself — the list endpoint labels every grant `manual`/`position`/`service_assignment` and
carries `revocable`).

## 3. Three findings, all from running the suite

### 3.1 🔴 The ceiling could not pass the commonest grant in the system

First run: **`company_admin` could not grant `member`.** Missing keys: `hr.case.cancel`,
`reports.appraisal.ack`, `reports.checkin.submit` — all three in `member`'s bundle because `member`
has SELF-SERVICE rules for them, and all three absent from `company_admin`'s bundle because nobody
cancels somebody else's HR case.

A plain subset test therefore forbids granting the baseline role to anyone, from any surface, forever
— and with it every role whose bundle contains a self-service key. This is the
`role-bundles-overstate-reach` family again: a bundle records what a role's rules NAME, while a
ceiling is a statement about authority over OTHER people.

**Fix:** the required set is now `bundle(role) − bundle(baseline 'member')`. `member` is one of the six
baseline roles every staff principal holds (`0095`), so nothing in its bundle is authority a grant can
add; everything above baseline still has to be held, so the ceiling keeps its point and stays
fail-closed for real authority.

⚠ **This changes an invariant P2-04 shipped.** It is done visibly, with the reasoning in the code, and
it needs architect/owner ratification. If rejected, the alternative is a catalog-level marker for
self-scoped keys — **not** a ceiling that cannot pass a `member` grant.

### 3.2 🔴 The same defect in the sensitive gate — and the flags behind it are unratified

Next run: a **dept head could not grant `member` either**, because the baseline role itself carries
**11 `sensitive`-flagged keys** (`core.client.delete`, `core.integration_connection.*`,
`hr.case.cancel`, …). §6.3.7's gate would therefore route the baseline grant — and every role above it
— as an override, i.e. refuse the entire dept-head surface.

Same subtraction applied. Worth stating plainly: **those `sensitive` flags have never been
owner-ratified** — PERMISSION-CONTRACT §9's sensitivity sign-off (now 107 keys) is still outstanding,
and this gate is the first thing in the program to depend on them. Whichever way that review lands the
subtraction stays necessary, but the *set* the gate acts on is currently un-reviewed data.

### 3.3 ⚠ The elevated fence is shadowed on this surface — no test can make it the deciding guard

Every role in `PHASE2_ELEVATED_FENCE` is refused by an EARLIER guard: `platform_admin`/
`group_executive` by the scope guard (global-only, and this surface refuses global), `client` by the
allow-list (`portal.*` is `uiGrantable:false`), and `owner` does not exist yet. So the fence is
genuine defence-in-depth here, not the first line — recorded in the test itself, because someone
simplifying one of the earlier checks would otherwise assume the fence still catches it.

## 4. What is deliberately NOT built (and why that is a refusal, not a gap in disguise)

**The routed override (§6.5).** It needs a `decide_override` literal action, which exists in neither
`resource_automation_approval.yaml` nor the permission catalog. Adding a literal action means a
catalog entry + a bundles migration + re-deriving the parity chain (catalog ↔ DB ↔ Cerbos ↔ UI mirror)
— its own ticket, not a side effect of this one.

So the surface **fails closed**: a dept head requesting a role with above-baseline sensitive keys gets
a typed `override_required` naming the missing mechanism and telling them to ask a company
administrator. company_admin and above are unaffected. Nothing is silently allowed, and nothing
pretends to be queued for an approval that cannot exist.

**Also still open, and now more load-bearing than before:** `assemblePrincipal()` does not filter on
`expires_at`. P2-09's sweep revokes expired grants and audits them, but between expiry and the next
tick the grant is live. The durable fix is one clause at resolution time
(`AND (expires_at IS NULL OR expires_at > now())`) and belongs with IAM-SEC-06's resolution-source
filter. Recorded, not assumed away.
