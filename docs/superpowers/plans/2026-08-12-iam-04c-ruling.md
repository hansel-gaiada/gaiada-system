# IAM-04c — RULING: may a `perm_*` arm be wired onto kinds carrying `org_unit_lead`?

**Question referred (owner-escalated):** does the `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` write-path constraint
close the "other-narrow" reachability direction well enough to wire a `perm_*` permission arm onto
`appraisal` / `report_document` (the two kinds naming `org_unit_lead`), by having
`permission-arm-hazard-scan.test.ts:722` consult that map the way the global-only assertion at `:707`
already does?

## VERDICT: **OPEN.** Do NOT wire. Keep `appraisal` and `report_document` deferred. Do NOT loosen the `:722` assertion.

This is ruling **(b)** — a real reachable write path remains — with **(c)** as the only durable route
to eventual closure (the write path is the wrong *layer*, so even fixing every writer would not by
itself make wiring safe under this program's own authority doctrine).

The referring engineer's hypothesis (the hazard is already closed; `:722` is stale and should consult
`ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS`) is **refuted on two independent grounds**, either one sufficient:

1. **The write-path constraint is not on all write paths.** `inviteUser` mints
   `org_unit_lead @ company` with no `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` check. The premise that `assignRole` is
   "the only unrestricted write path" (its own code comment, `admin-identity.controller.ts:322`) is
   **false**. Concrete, reachable, named below.
2. **A write-path guard is the wrong layer to license a Cerbos decision.** `CLAUDE.md` fixes Cerbos +
   RLS as the authorization authority and every controller as a mirror/fallback. The `:722` gate and
   both affected policy files' own comments explicitly frame themselves as *defense-in-depth against*
   the write-path guard, not reliant on it. Making the Cerbos-side gate depend on an app-layer
   controller being correct **and complete** inverts that — and we just proved it is not complete.

Bias-toward-OPEN was not needed to reach this; finding (1) is dispositive on its own.

## 1. Mechanism — confirmed from source, not from the summary

The over-grant chain, each link verified:

| Link | Evidence | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Grant → perm scope | `platform-nest/src/rbac/principal.ts:100-107` | The `perms` query `SELECT DISTINCT p.key, ur.scope_type, ur.scope_id … JOIN role_permissions … JOIN permissions` copies the **grant row's** `scope_type`/`scope_id` verbatim onto every resolved permission. A `company`-scoped grant yields `company`-scoped perms. |
| Perm → PDP | `platform-nest/src/rbac/cerbos.ts:60` | `principalPayload` ships `attr.perms = [{key, scopeType, scopeId}]` to Cerbos on every check. |
| Mirror shape | `cerbos/policies/derived_roles.yaml` (`perm_pm_task_read` and all 3 sanctioned batches) | Every `perm_*` derived role matches `g.scopeType == "global" || (g.scopeType == "company" && g.scopeId == tenantId)`. A `company`-scoped perm at the resource's tenant **matches**. |
| `org_unit_lead` legit scope | `derived_roles.yaml:89-95` | Its role-arm condition is `g.scopeType == "org_unit" && g.scopeId in unitAncestors` — **org_unit only**. A legitimately org_unit-scoped grant yields org_unit-scoped perms, which the mirror's two branches can **never** match. The exploit therefore **requires** an `org_unit_lead` grant recorded at `company` or `global` scope — exactly what a correct write guard should forbid. |
| Blast radius bound | role bundle (`src/rbac/role-permission-bundles.json`, read live this session) | `org_unit_lead`'s bundle is **exactly two keys**: `reports.appraisal.read`, `reports.document.read_department`. So a mis-scoped `org_unit_lead` grant can only ever over-reach on *those two* actions — but at **company scope** (whole company), collapsing the org-unit subtree boundary its role-arm rule exists to enforce. |

So the hazard is precise: wiring `perm_report_document_read_department` (or `perm_appraisal_read`) on
its kind lets a `company`/`global`-scoped `org_unit_lead` grant read that action **across the whole
company**, flattening the subtree cascade `resource_report_document.yaml:105-119` /
`resource_appraisal.yaml:123-139` deliberately impose. This is the exact harm the B5 brief named.

`assemblePrincipal`'s answer to the owner's direct question: **yes, a mis-scoped grant yields
company-scoped perms** (link 1 above). That is the whole engine of the hazard.

**On seeds/migrations bypassing the guard:** they do bypass it (direct SQL, owner/migrator role), and
that is **acceptable** *only because* every one of them hardcodes a correct (role, scope) pair —
`personas.ts` grants `org_unit_lead` at `org_unit`, the portal seed grants `client` at `company`.
None can emit a mis-scoped constrained-role grant. So they are not the hole. **`inviteUser` is.**

### The one unguarded writer, exercised end-to-end

- **Principal:** a `company_admin` of company `X` (holds `user:create` in-tenant —
  `resource_user.yaml:18-21`; there are 11 live `company_admin` grants per §3). Works identically for
  `platform_admin`/`group_executive`.
- **Call:** `POST /api/X/users` (`inviteUser`) with
  `{ email: <target E's email>, name: "…", roleId: <org_unit_lead's global role id> }`.
  `inviteUser` resolves/creates `E`, upserts an active membership, then executes
  `INSERT INTO user_roles(…, scope_type, scope_id) VALUES (…, 'company', X)` at line 252 — **never
  consulting `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS`.** The role picker (`:94-114`) even lists `org_unit_lead`, and
  the API accepts the id directly regardless.
- **Grant produced:** `org_unit_lead @ company:X` — the scope `org_unit_lead`'s own condition can
  never satisfy; the row `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` is meant to make "structurally impossible".
- **Resolution:** `assemblePrincipal(E)` → `perms = [{reports.appraisal.read, company, X},
  {reports.document.read_department, company, X}]`.
- **The over-grant, the moment a mirror is wired:** `perm_report_document_read_department` (a
  Pattern-A/B-clean action the B5 report says "would otherwise have been mirrorable") lets `E` read
  `read_department` for **every** department in `X` — KPIs and appraisal-band inputs
  (`resource_report_document.yaml` header) for the whole company. `E` is an arbitrary employee, not a
  company_admin, not HR, not a dept lead. Same for `perm_appraisal_read` on `appraisal`.

The grant can be conferred on **any target** (a plain member), or on the caller themselves. This is
reachable **today**, through `inviteUser`, **with `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` fully in place** — because
the guard is simply not on that path.

## 4. Is `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` complete?

**Against the current policy: yes.** The scan flags exactly four scope-constrained roles —
`platform_admin`, `group_executive` (global-only), `client` (company-only), `org_unit_lead`
(org_unit-only) — and the map (`admin-identity.controller.ts:48-53`) has exactly those four.
`module_staff/manager/approver` are correctly excluded (their hazard is a resource-attribute gate,
not a scope constraint).

**But its completeness is only half machine-checked.** `permission-arm-hazard-scan.test.ts:707`
verifies the *global-only* entries are present (via `loadGlobalOnlyRolesFromController`). **Nothing**
asserts that `client` and `org_unit_lead` are in the map, nor that the map covers *every*
scope-constrained role the sweep finds in *both* directions. So the other-narrow half of the map is
maintained by hand — the eighth hand-maintained list the file itself warns about, only half-closed.
A new company-only or org-unit-only role added tomorrow would silently fall out of the guard with no
test going red. (Fix listed in §8.)

## 5. Is the kind-scoped granularity load-bearing? (owner's question)

**Yes — kind-scoping is a *sound* over-approximation, and the naive action-scoped alternative would be
*unsound*.** Reasoning:

- `org_unit_lead`'s hazard is **Pattern C** (its own clean, scope-narrow rule), not Pattern A or B.
  An action-scoped check built on Pattern-A/B co-occurrence — the shape Part 3's guards use — would
  **miss it entirely**, because `org_unit_lead` never mixes into another rule and never coexists with
  a self-scoped rule on the same action. So "just check the action" is not safe here.
- The *precise* poisoned set is "actions whose key is in a mis-scopable role's bundle" — for
  `org_unit_lead`, exactly `{appraisal.read, report_document.read_department}`. A correct action-scoped
  gate would have to additionally intersect each scope-constrained role's **bundle** with the action
  being wired. That is real extra machinery (re-derive the bundle, intersect per action) and a new
  fragile dependency on bundle contents.
- Kind-scoping sidesteps all of that by conservatively blocking **any** mirror on a kind that hosts a
  scope-narrow role. It never lets an exploit through; it only over-defers *clean* actions
  (`read_project`/`read_company` on `report_document`; `cycle_admin`/`finalize`/`write`/`submit` on
  `appraisal` — none of which are in `org_unit_lead`'s bundle, so none are actually exploitable via
  it). That over-deferral is the measured cost, and on appraisal data it is the correct bias.

**Recommendation: keep the kind-scoped gate.** If the clean actions are worth reclaiming later, the
sound way is a bundle-aware action-scoped check *plus* a source-level fix (§8), not loosening `:722`.

## 6. Is the global-only direction actually sound? (owner's question) — NO, same gap

The engineer noted the two directions are "asymmetric only because one was updated and the other was
not," implying the global-only side is the sound reference. **It is not the sound reference — it shares
the identical hole.** `inviteUser` mints `platform_admin @ company:X` (247-perm bundle) or
`group_executive @ company:X` (125-perm bundle) with the same no-guard path. Those two roles **already
have wired mirrors across ~30 kinds today** — `resource_user.yaml:30-45` (`perm_user_*`) alone lets a
company-scoped `core.user.*` perm through, plus `company`/`contract`/`invoice`/`hr_record`/
`service_assignment`/`agency`/`knowledge`/`webdev`/`search`. So a `company_admin` can escalate an
arbitrary user — **or themselves** — to `platform_admin`-equivalent permission reach inside their own
tenant, via `inviteUser`. That is the exact IAM-SEC-02 self-escalation defect `GLOBAL_ONLY_ROLES`
claimed to close, still reachable through the un-guarded co-writer, and it is **live wherever the
IAM-04 policy set is deployed with `attr.perms` populated** (which `cerbos.ts:60` always does).

Severity caveat, stated honestly: the B5 report asserts the perm arm is "not yet load-bearing" on the
estate. I verified the *code state* (policies wired, `attr.perms` sent); I did **not** verify which
policy bundle the live Cerbos is currently serving (and `CLAUDE.md`: *health ≠ current policy*). So I
flag this as a **live-when-deployed** code-state defect and recommend a devops/QA check of the live
policy bundle + a `platform_admin@company` probe, rather than asserting it fires on
`erp.gaiada.online` this minute.

**Consequence for the ruling:** the right fix tightens **both** directions and does **not** treat the
global-only side as a safe precedent to copy. If anything, the referral surfaced that the global-only
"CLOSED" status was itself overstated.

## 7. Does this widen access to HR appraisal data, and who gains?

- **Today (no mirror on either kind): no widening.** `appraisal`/`report_document` are decided by
  role-name matching only; a mis-scoped `org_unit_lead` grant is inert (its perms match no mirror; its
  role-name grant fails the `org_unit`-only condition). The deferral is holding. The danger is entirely
  in the *act of wiring*.
- **If wired (the proposed change): yes.** A `company_admin` could confer, on **any employee**,
  company-wide `report_document.read_department` and `appraisal.read` — i.e. department-grain report
  documents and every employee's appraisal record across the whole company, to someone entitled only
  to their own subtree (or, as a plain member, only their own). For a `company_admin` *self-target* it
  is a no-op (they already hold `appraisal.read` via DR-5 and all `report_document` grains), but for
  any other grantee it is a genuine gain of the most sensitive HR data in the system. This is why a
  wrong CLOSED here is expensive and the deferral must hold.

## 8. What must be TRUE for it to be CLOSED — to pin as tests (if closure is pursued later)

Closure does **not** come from "trust the write path." The durable options, cheapest-sound first:

- **(A) Fix at the resolution source (recommended).** Make `assemblePrincipal()` (or 0094's bundling)
  refuse to emit a perm whose grant `(role, scopeType)` pairing is invalid — drop any perm resolved
  through a grant at a scope the role's own condition can't satisfy. Then a mis-scoped grant yields
  **no** poisoned perm, regardless of how the row was created (`inviteUser`, direct DB, pre-existing).
  This closes it at the authority boundary, matching `CLAUDE.md`. Cost: `assemblePrincipal` gains the
  same role→allowed-scope data (`ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS`, applied at *resolve* time), one filter;
  machine-check it against `derived_roles.yaml` exactly as the hazard scan already does for the
  controller map.
- **(B) Cerbos-side exclusion.** Give each affected `perm_*` mirror a witness clause
  `!request.principal.attr.grants.exists(g, g.role=="org_unit_lead" && g.scopeType != "org_unit")`
  (the `team_lead`×`pm_task` pattern, generalised to "this scope-narrow role held at a wrong scope"),
  and extend Part 3's grants-exclusion guard to assert its presence in the other-narrow direction.
  Cost: per-kind/per-role clauses, fragile to author.
- **(C) Leave permanently unwired** (B5's option c). `appraisal`/`report_document` keep role-name
  matching forever. Cost: zero; acceptable given sensitivity.

**Independently of the wiring decision, close the `inviteUser` gap** (route its optional role grant
through the same `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` check, or through `assignRole`'s logic). That is a
straight-up bug fix; it also re-closes the global-only IAM-SEC-02 hole from §6.

Invariants to pin as tests **before** any future wiring is allowed:

1. `assemblePrincipal` emits **zero** perms for a synthetic `org_unit_lead @ company` grant (proves
   the source-level filter, option A).
2. `ROLE_SCOPE_CONSTRAINTS` is machine-checked **complete against every scope-constrained role in both
   directions** — extend the scan so `:722`'s neighbourhood asserts `client` and `org_unit_lead` are in
   the map (mirror of `:707`), closing the half-open completeness check (§4).
3. Every `user_roles` writer that accepts a caller-chosen role is scope-guarded — extend
   `managed-by-invariant.test.ts` (which already enumerates writers) to assert scope-guard coverage, so
   a *third* unguarded writer cannot silently reappear.
4. The kind-scoped `:722` gate stays red for `appraisal`/`report_document` until (A) or (B) is proven
   — belt-and-suspenders, per the program's defense-in-depth doctrine.

Until at least invariant 1 (or B) exists **and** the `inviteUser` writer is guarded, the honest state
is: **the mirror cannot be wired safely.**
